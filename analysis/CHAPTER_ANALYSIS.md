# CHAPTER ANALYSIS
### What the current archive can and cannot contribute to each chapter

Based on 414 photographs, every one of them visually reviewed.
Nothing below is inferred from filenames alone.

The archive is **back-loaded**. Nearly two thirds of it covers the last six
years. The years that make the ending mean something — the years when it was
just the two of them — rest on about a hundred scanned prints.

---

## CHAPTER 1 — BEFORE WE KNEW

**Can the archive carry it? Yes, and better than expected.**

63 photographs land in 2003–2006, plus more in the immediately following years,
almost all from the scanned prints. **48 of them show the couple alone.** This is
the single richest vein of couple material in the entire project, and it is the
chapter most people would expect to be empty.

Standouts: a student-room frame where he has swept her off her feet mid-laugh
(`S037`); a kiss caught at the edge of a party with no audience (`S026`); a
laughing companion frame to it (`S028`); a wine toast in a borrowed cabin
(`S029`); the couple in front of a house they clearly wanted photographed
(`S036`).

**An unresolved discovery.** Burned-in camera date stamps on the earliest prints
read `00. 5.10`, `00. 4.23` and `00. 5.14` — spring **2000**. The seasonal
content agrees (bare April trees, a May formal). That is roughly three years
before the "together since 2003" in the brief, and both of you appear in them.
Either you go back further than the project's framing assumes, or these are
pre-relationship photos from the same circle. **This needs your answer**, because
"23 YEARS OF US" is on screen in the closing text and the archive may be
arguing for a bigger number.

---

## CHAPTER 2 — THE DAY WE SAID I DO

**Can the archive carry it? No. Not at all.**

**There are zero photographs of your wedding in the archive.** Not a gown, not a
veil, not a tuxedo, not a ceremony, not a reception, not a first dance. Two
frames show weddings — `S106` and `S091` — but those are *other people's*, where
she is a bridesmaid in black satin and he wears a groomsman's boutonnière.

Both independent reviews of all 110 scanned prints reached this conclusion
separately.

This is the largest single gap in the project. The chapter is currently
unmakeable. It is also the chapter the whole film is named for: the wedding is
the event the anniversary commemorates, and the "somehow that was already 15
years ago" line has nothing to sit under.

**Nearest substitutes, if the photos truly do not exist:** the most formal
couple portrait in the archive (`S098`, 2008–2011, a wedding-adjacent occasion),
or `S100` (2010–2013, the best-exposed couple portrait of that era). Both are
compromises and neither reads as a wedding.

## CHAPTER 3 — JUST THE TWO OF US

**Can the archive carry it? Partly.**

22 photographs in 2006–2010 and 18 in 2010–2013. Thin, but the quality is high
and it is nearly all couple material: 19 and 10 couple-alone frames respectively.

The strongest run is hard-dated by burned-in stamps to 2007 — `03.17.2007`
(St. Patrick's, a green "GOT BEER?" hat), `05.11.2007`, `05.19.2007`,
`05.25.2007`, `05.27.2007`, `07.04.2007`, `08.05.2007`. A single well-documented
year gives this chapter a spine.

Also here: his graduation with the ceremony banner legible (`S095`) — the
biggest documented milestone in the pre-children archive; a snowstorm frame with
both laughing and snow visibly on his head (`S083`); and the Atlantis resort in
the Bahamas (`S110`, `S072`, `S074`), which may be the honeymoon. **Please
confirm** — if it is, those photographs belong in Chapter 2 and would partially
rescue it.

**What is missing:** an engagement, a first home interior, a pregnancy.

---

## CHAPTER 4 — THEN THERE WERE FIVE

**Can the archive carry it? Yes — the births are exceptionally well covered.**

All three arrivals are documented:

- **Daughter one, 2013-11-01** — `D001`, hospital, newborn in both their arms.
- **Daughter two, 2016-11-01** — `D013`, and then `D014`, a black-and-white
  hospital frame of all four together for the first time. `D014` is the single
  strongest emotional photograph in the archive.
- **Daughter three, ~Sept 2020** — `D241`, the going-home frame: hospital bed,
  bow-wrapped newborn in the car seat, **both parents in face masks**. That
  photograph dates itself and carries the whole era in one image.

Both older daughters' hospital photographs are dated **November 1st, three years
apart**. If that is real and not an EXIF artifact, the chapter can open on one
and land on the other in near-identical framing. **Worth confirming.**

Supporting material is strong: the older sister meeting the baby (`D015`); the
father holding the newborn while the big sister holds her doll in the same pose
(`D016`); the visibly pregnant beach portrait weeks before the third arrives
(`D085`); and the newborn's first Halloween shot from directly overhead with all
four laid out in a row (`D087`).

**The weak seam** is 2013–2016 — only 10 photographs covering the entire period
when they first became parents. The transition from couple to family is
documented at its endpoints but barely in between.

## CHAPTER 5 — THE CHAOS

**Can the archive carry it? Yes, and with unusual honesty.**

The archive is rich in the imperfect and the funny, which most family archives
are not:

- `D066` (2020-03-30) — the girls' hands pressed flat against a glass storm
  door with an older grey-haired man kept outside on the walk. A pandemic
  doorway visit. Historically the most eloquent frame in the archive.
- `D180` — the girls covering their father's shaved head in gem stickers, hands
  still working. Unrepeatable.
- `D020` — dad sprawled in a lawn chair, a daughter in a tiny chair beside him
  copying his exact pose.
- `D010` — both parents dressed formally, a small daughter photobombing at knee
  height.
- `D019` — the double stroller and the coffee. The logistics years, plainly.

Masks recur through 2020–2022 (`D241`, `D260` at the Rockefeller tree,
`D262`), which gives the chapter a real spine rather than a vague mood.

## CHAPTER 6 — THE LIFE WE BUILT

**Can the archive carry it? Easily — the risk here is over-supply.**

The dominant threads:

- **Lacrosse.** It runs from Jr. Lions through club and travel teams and
  dominates 2024–2026. The family surname is legible on the jerseys in `D212`,
  `D213`, `D216`.
- **The annual beach portrait**, which doubles as a growth chart of the family.
- **A major resort trip, Jul 30 – Aug 3 2023** — the single richest visual run
  in the archive: `D122` (all three girls draped over their father while he
  reads), `D131` (dusk, carrying the youngest along a dock), `D138` (three
  sisters tangled on empty sand at last light).
- **The Christmas cycle** — tree farm, Santa, matching pyjamas, Rockefeller
  Center.
- A First Communion–style milestone on 2025-05-03 (`D163`) — white dress,
  church steps. Reads as one, unconfirmed from the frame.

**The danger:** this chapter could swallow the film. It has more strong material
than Chapters 1–4 combined, and it is the least important to the emotional
argument. It should be the most ruthlessly cut.

---

## CHAPTER 7 — TODAY

**Can the archive carry it? Yes for the family. Marginally for the couple.**

132 photographs, 22 family-of-five frames (all 2020 or later). The best
present-day family images: `D263` (all five on the playroom floor in holiday
pyjamas, every face genuinely lit up), `D283` (four faces filling the frame,
all laughing), `D216`/`D217` (the three sisters from behind in identical
family-name jerseys, tallest to smallest).

**The problem is the ending.** The film is supposed to close on the strongest
photograph of the two of you — and recent couple photographs are scarce. See the
gap analysis below.

## THE STRUCTURAL PROBLEM

Couple-alone photographs, by era:

| Era | Couple-alone photos |
|---|---:|
| 2003–2006 | 48 |
| 2006–2010 | 19 |
| 2010–2013 | 10 |
| 2013–2016 | **1** |
| 2016–2020 | **2** |
| 2020–2023 | 7 |
| 2023–2026 | 8 |

**77 of the 98 couple photographs in this archive were taken before your first
daughter was born.** After 2013 the two of you very nearly vanish from your own
record — one photograph in three years, then two in four more.

This is the most common thing that happens to a family archive, and it is
exactly the thing this film is about. It cuts both ways:

- **As a problem:** the closing image — "I'd still choose you" over the
  strongest photograph of the two of you — currently has only a handful of
  recent candidates. The leaders are `D300` (formally dressed on a deck over a
  river valley, visibly older) and `D111` (warm low sun, treeline gone gold,
  the tightest and most unguarded recent frame). `D018` (2017, sunglasses,
  cheek to cheek) is the strongest of the middle years.
- **As material:** the disappearance is itself true, and the film can say so.
  The density of couple photographs collapsing exactly when the children arrive
  is the visual proof of the voiceover line about years spent "just trying to
  keep up with everything."
