# PHOTO HUNT
### What to look for when you dig through the rest of the physical archive

You have already digitized 110 prints, and they changed the project — the early
years turned out to be the **best**-covered couple material you have, not the
worst. This list is about what is still missing.

Ordered by how much each would improve the film. Nothing here assumes the
photographs exist. If they don't, say so and the story adapts.

---

## PRIORITY 1 — THE WEDDING. Everything else is secondary.

**There is not one photograph of your wedding in the archive.** No gown, no veil,
no tuxedo, no ceremony, no reception, no first dance, no rings. Two independent
reviews of all 110 scans confirmed it separately.

This is a 15th **wedding** anniversary film with no wedding in it. Chapter 2
currently cannot be made.

Look for, in rough order of value:

1. **The two of you at the altar or during the ceremony** — the single most
   important missing photograph in this project.
2. **The first dance.**
3. **Any portrait of the two of you alone in wedding clothes.**
4. **Her getting ready** — the dress, her mother, her sisters or friends.
5. **You waiting, or seeing her for the first time.**
6. **The exit** — sparklers, car, rice, whatever it was.
7. **The full wedding party**, for identifying who was there.
8. **Anything at all with the date on it** — a program, a printed menu, a
   signed card, the cake.

Where to look beyond your own boxes: **your parents and her parents almost
certainly have a wedding album.** So do the bridal party. A professional
photographer may still hold the originals — 2011 is recent enough that the files
may exist on a disc or a studio archive. That single album would fix the
largest hole in this film in one afternoon.

## PRIORITY 2 — The engagement

Nothing in the archive marks it. Look for:

- The proposal itself, or the photograph taken minutes afterwards.
- The first picture of her wearing the ring, or a photo of her hand.
- Any engagement party or announcement.
- A formal engagement portrait, if you had one taken.

This is the hinge between Chapters 1 and 2 and it is currently blank.

## PRIORITY 3 — The honeymoon (possibly already found — please confirm)

Several prints show the two of you at the **Atlantis resort in the Bahamas**
(`S110`, `S072`, `S074`), plus other beach and marina frames. If any of those
are the honeymoon, tell me and Chapter 2 partially recovers immediately.

If the honeymoon was somewhere else, look for it. Look particularly for anything
where she is still visibly a bride — a "Mrs." bag, a hotel welcome, resort
staff making a fuss.

## PRIORITY 4 — The first home

`S036` shows the two of you in front of a house you clearly wanted photographed,
and one earlier scan shows a house exterior with no people. That is nearly all
there is.

Look for:

- The two of you **inside** the first place you lived together — the actual
  rooms, not the exterior.
- Moving day. Boxes, an empty room, a mattress on the floor.
- Keys, a closing, a "sold" sign.
- The first Christmas tree in your own place. (One early scan already shows a
  tree in a room that doesn't match later houses — worth identifying.)

## PRIORITY 5 — Pregnancy and becoming parents, 2013

The archive jumps from the couple straight to a newborn in a hospital room.
The nine months before are absent.

Look for:

- Any pregnancy photograph from the **first** pregnancy — a bump, a bare belly,
  a maternity portrait, an ultrasound printout.
- A baby shower.
- The nursery before she arrived.
- The drive to the hospital, the waiting, the labour hours.
- Coming home for the first time, and the car seat in the door.

You have this coverage for the **third** daughter (`D085`, `D241`) and almost
none for the first. The first is the one the story needs — it's the moment the
couple becomes parents.

## PRIORITY 6 — 2013 to 2016, the thinnest years in the whole archive

Only **10 photographs** exist for this entire period, and only **one** shows the
two of you alone in three years.

Look for anything at all from these years, but especially:

- **The two of you together**, with or without the baby.
- The first daughter's first birthday, first steps, first day anywhere.
- Ordinary days at home. Not events — Tuesdays.

---

## PRIORITY 7 — The two of you, at any point after 2013

77 of your 98 couple photographs predate your first child. After that you nearly
disappear from your own archive: one photograph in 2013–2016, two in 2016–2020.

Look for anything where you are together and the children are not the subject:

- Date nights, weddings you attended, anniversaries.
- Any anniversary photograph from any year — especially the 10th.
- Vacations without the children.
- Bad phone photos of the two of you on a couch. These count. They may matter
  more than the good ones.

---

## PRIORITY 8 — Her, on her own

The archive has plenty of her with the children and few photographs of her as
herself. For a film addressed to her, that matters.

Look for portraits of her from every era — and specifically for the ones **you**
think are the most beautiful photographs of her ever taken. Those are a
different selection than the ones an archivist would make, and I can't make it
for you.

## PRIORITY 9 — The lacrosse thread

One scanned print shows **a women's lacrosse game around 2000** (`S006`/`S014`,
date-stamped `00. 4.23`), and the modern archive is full of your daughters
playing lacrosse in family-name jerseys.

If that early player is her, this is the best structural rhyme available to the
film — the same sport, the same field posture, twenty-five years apart. Look for
more of her playing: team photos, action shots, a uniform, a school jersey.

**Please confirm whether that is her.** If it is, it earns a deliberate cut in
the final edit.

---

## PRIORITY 10 — Fill the funny and the imperfect

Chapter 5 works because it is honest. The best material found so far is a lawn
chair, a photobomb, and a head covered in gem stickers.

Look for: bad haircuts, disastrous holidays, cars that died, tiny apartments,
anything where you both look terrible and happy. Blurry and badly framed is
fine. Do not filter these out — they are the texture the film needs and they are
the first thing people throw away.

## A note on scanning

Everything you scanned came through Google PhotoScan, which stamps the file with
**the time you scanned it**, not when the photo was taken. That's handled — the
pipeline detects it and dates those photos visually instead.

Two things that would help a lot on the next batch:

1. **Don't crop out the borders.** Several prints carry burned-in orange date
   stamps in the corner, and those are the only hard dates in the entire
   pre-2013 archive. They are worth more than a tidy edge.
2. **Scan the backs** of any print with writing on it. A pencilled date or a
   name on the reverse is the best dating evidence there is, and it's invisible
   once the print is scanned face-only.
